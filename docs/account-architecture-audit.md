# Account architecture reconstruction and implementation audit

Status: source reconstruction and code audit recorded **before the implementation changes in this branch** (2026-09-29). This is a map of intended distinctions, implemented behavior, and open decisions; it does not convert a proposal or a UI mock into a grant of authority. The source pack's decision and conflict registers, direct August 7 owner brief (`SRC-0026`), transfer-kit D-008/D-009, August 30 retrospective (`SRC-0048`), May 31 owner brief (`SRC-0032`), AccessLobby 1/2 excerpts, UI chat/images, MVP steps/history, and the repository's ADRs/contracts were checked. The August 30 retrospective and AccessLobby excerpts are secondary syntheses; `03_CONFIRMED_DECISIONS.md` and `15_UNRESOLVED_QUESTIONS.md` distinguish the owner-confirmed boundaries from their suggested details. UI examples are illustrative, not account data or permission policies.

## Terms and cardinalities

| Object | Nature, owner, and relationships | Status of authority |
|---|---|---|
| Person/human identity | One natural person has a durable AccessLobby person ID and can have zero or more `(issuer, subject)` IAM links. Email/phone/credential changes must not change the person ID. Keycloak authenticates; AccessLobby maps the verified subject. A person can act personally, join many organizations, and link to local accounts in many peers. | Confirmed DEC-003/004, D-009; first issuer mapping implemented. Duplicate-person merge, cross-realm identity and extra credential links are undecided Q-001/002/009. |
| Personal account/context | The person's own participation, available independently of organizational membership. It is **not** a second human identity or a separate legal entity. | Confirmed DEC-004; implemented as the personal context. |
| Organization | A separate legal or operating entity, never a human login. Company/business, supplier, farm, school, agency, manufacturer, retailer, logistics company, nonprofit and professional organization are examples of the umbrella, not nine approved credential classes. One organization can have many human memberships; one person can belong to many organizations. Several organizations may represent businesses a person works with. | Umbrella and multi-membership confirmed. Flat, self-asserted organization with active/suspended status implemented provisionally; legal kind, verification, parent/child, cross-border tenancy and ownership proof remain Q-004/012/026. |
| Organization membership | Association of a person with one organization and a contextual administration role; acceptance requires an invitation in the present provisional model. A member's relationship may end while the human identity persists. Multiple owners are possible; the last active owner cannot leave. Membership does **not** confer app entry or peer resource permission. | Confirmed separation DEC-004/005; provisional `owner`, `administrator`, `member` implemented by ADR 006. No legal-controller claim follows from an `owner` row. |
| Organizational subdivision | Department, team, branch, location, business unit, subsidiary or workspace beneath or beside an organization; staff can have different scope in each. | Source-intended structural distinction (`SRC-0048` §§9–11), but cardinalities, inherited rights, tenant boundary and canonical owner remain Q-004/010. No schema or grants yet. Do not flatten one into a verified organization or infer parent access. |
| Acting context | Person acting for themselves or selecting one active organization; changing context preserves the human principal. Different personal, organization and app roles can coexist. Selecting a context never becomes a credential or delegation by itself. | Confirmed concept; first-party sealed display choice and peer read-only membership choice provisionally implemented. Cross-app context/authority token remains Q-006/010/019. |
| Peer application and local account | An OIDC client integrates with AccessLobby; each peer may own one or more local profile/account relationships for the human and organization. Local mapping is explicitly bound after authentication/proof of an existing account. The peer owns seller, buyer, supplier, driver, instructor, warehouse worker and other domain personas, local roles, permissions and protected resources. | Confirmed DEC-005/006/009. Reference peer illustrates local mapping and grant; real peer adapters and durable legacy linking absent. An OIDC client ID is an application, not a human or an app admission grant. |
| Application admission/grant | Separate broad decision that a person or organization may enter an app, which may be discoverable or invite-gated. It must be evaluated in addition to membership; detailed resource authorization stays in that app. Subscription, seat, license, payment and verification may influence it without becoming the same object. | Direction confirmed DEC-009 and `SRC-0026` §§8, 11–12. Exact grant owner, lifecycle, audience, policy, billing projection and eligibility are Q-005/019/022. **No central grant engine exists.** |
| Agent/service-provider relationship | A person or organization acts as a service provider or representative for another; agent, contractor, procurement, sourcing, warehouse and delivery labels may be peer-local personas. True on-behalf-of delegation needs sponsor, scope, expiry, effective authority and original actor. | Distinction appears in May brief and `SRC-0048` §5; exact delegation remains Q-006. Do not equate an organization membership or job title with delegated power. |
| Non-human actor | Service principal, workload, API client, integration, background worker, automation/AI agent or independently enrolled device is separate from the human sign-in model. It may be sponsored by a human/organization and must have bounded audience, scope, credentials and audit lineage. A trusted *human login device* is a different relationship. | Intended later direction; credential issuer, ownership, onboarding, delegated actions and revocation unresolved Q-007/019/021. There is no non-human account API. Existing infrastructure credentials/OIDC client configuration do not make these human accounts. |
| Guest or discovered third party | Anonymous/guest peer state, and an externally discovered business/facility not yet claimed, are not authenticated AccessLobby people or organizations. A later claim/link must prove control and preserve provenance. | Guest-first and explicit peer link direction; exact guest/WordPress transition Q-017, discovered-entity claim Q-026. |
| Informal group/household | Family or informal group might require a separate consent/safeguarding model; not automatically a business tenant or credential. | Historical example only; Q-004. |

An `owner` membership grants the limited provisional organization-management actions in ADR 006. A legal controller, platform operator, billing administrator, security administrator, delegated administrator, department manager, employee or auditor is **not** automatically that owner. The source lists those roles as candidates or peer/domain roles (`SRC-0026` §10; `SRC-0048` §§8–12, 89–92), without a universal permission lattice. Neither a global `admin=true` nor an organization `role` token is an approved substitute.

## Lifecycle and workspace by participant

| Participant/account relationship | Entry, linking and routing | Continuing access, change and exit | Current state before this branch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual human / personal | Pilot provisioning or enabled self-registration in Keycloak, credential challenge and OIDC code/PKCE; API verifies issuer/audience/client/signed token and resolves stable person ID. The personal context exists on first resolution. | First-party account view, identity ID, personal selection; peer entry requires that peer's own account/admission. Keycloak handles credential reset/verification when configured; first-party status `suspended` denies API resolution. Contact changes, duplicate merge, deletion/retention and full recovery policy open. | **Partly implemented**: real sign-in, person mapping, sessions and personal view; callback opens a cookie before API person resolution; all callbacks land on `/account`. Live email/recovery and suspension administration unqualified. |
| Creator / provisional organization owner | Authenticated person creates a self-asserted organization and becomes its first administration owner; no legal claim, business verification or app entitlement is implied. May create or join several separate organizations. | View roster, invite active person IDs, assign owner/admin/member, revoke invitations, remove members, transfer by adding another owner, select context; last-active-owner guard. Exit requires another owner. Legal controller validation, entity suspension/closure and ownership recovery open. | **Partly implemented**: API, UI and DB lifecycle exist; generic landing/account dashboard hides organization until `/contexts`; ownership is provisional. |
| Invited administrator/member/staff representative | Owner invites an existing active person by exact ID; admin can invite ordinary members. Intended recipient signs in, sees seven-day invitation, accepts/declines, then can select org context. New people must first register and convey their ID by trusted channel. | Owner/admin/member powers are scoped to this org. Admin manages ordinary membership only; member sees roster, can leave. Owner can promote/demote; removal/leave revokes membership context on the next live check. Staff/department/peer persona requires separate scope and peer-local onboarding. | **Partly implemented**: invitation, role, leave/remove, audit and read-only peer projection; no contact invitation, delivery, org subaccounts, organization-aware post-login routing or app admission. |
| Multiple entities/roles/accounts for one human | One verified principal can have personal context, several organizations with different roles and several peer-local accounts. Explicit peer legacy linking proves both accounts, never email equality. A different *human account* needs a new IAM authentication, not a cookie/context change. | Choose a context and recheck live membership; peer independently checks its local grant. Revoked/suspended org excluded from projection. Account switch must establish a different authenticated human; eventual cross-realm and cross-app switching semantics open. | **Partly implemented**: membership switch and reference-peer display/local denial work; first-party explicit human-account switch and reliable post-login choice absent. Reference peer stores links and sessions only in memory. |
| App account: default-discoverable or invite-gated | App appears/discovers only under its visibility policy; authenticated person must receive the applicable broad entry grant and, where applicable, organization association. Peer may then run its own onboarding. | Broad app grant can end independently of membership; peer checks its own resource/action rules and session. Subscription/verification/seat may be inputs only under an agreed contract. | **Missing centrally**: OIDC allowlist/client registration exists, but it is not an app directory, admission registry or grant lifecycle; reference peer supplies a local protected-resource grant. |
| Agent, legal controller, delegated/billing/security admin | Establish verified authority and original actor, sponsor, permitted org/app actions, approval and step-up before any privilege; no role by claimed title. | Bounded authority, audit, recovery, revocation and transfer; an app may separately model agent-specific resources. | **Unspecified/missing**: no approved verification/delegation/role policy; implementing powers now would invent authority. |
| Machine/service/AI/device | Register separate principal/credential with accountable sponsor, audience/scope, expiry and rotation; a device might instead be bound to a human session. | Audit actor type and delegation chain; revoke without suspending the sponsor, deny unrelated tenant and high-risk action. | **Unspecified/missing**: human-only v0.1 contract and no credential/grant design. |
| Guest, legacy customer/staff, discovered external entity, household | Guest browses in peer; existing peer user links only after dual proof; discovered entity needs later claim review; household would need safeguards. | Peer-local permissions and history remain local; no automatic merge/claim by email or name. | **Partial sample only** for peer linking; household and claim models missing and undecided. |

`SRC-0026` §9 makes person-first onboarding authoritative; §8 distinguishes invite-gated from default-discoverable admission; §§10–12 request role/entitlement analysis rather than dictating a final role table. `SRC-0048` §§83–90 sketches more lifecycle states and recovery, but Source Pack Q-004/005/020/021 says their transitions are not settled. The UI mock's profile/verification/security pages are not evidence of live consent, proofing, or account permissions.

### Changes justified without deciding open policy

1. Resolve the human and load their first-party contexts **during** callback before issuing the AccessLobby web session. Fail closed on API unavailability/suspension and avoid a half-created local session. Route personal-only people to their account and people with organization memberships or pending invitations to the choice/workspace page. No organization is silently made active.
2. Make the first-party account screen show the actual person's organization roles and pending invitations, linking to existing scoped organization controls. A missing context service must be shown as unavailable rather than interpreted as personal-only.
3. Provide an explicit fresh-human sign-in action via the IAM challenge (`prompt=login`), clearing selected organization on completed authentication. Organization context switching continues to use the same human principal.
4. Hold an organization row lock through the roster/detail read so removal and suspension cannot race disclosure after the membership check.

These changes do **not** settle legal controllers, verified organization claims, app admission, non-human credentials, hierarchy, entitlement/payment, account merge or retention. They need owner decisions and separate versioned contracts before behavior can be claimed.

## Implemented in this branch and verification

| Change | Evidence and present limit |
|---|---|
| Post-login person/context resolution | The callback now calls `/v1/me` and `/v1/contexts` with its fresh access token before setting the web cookie. It checks the v0.1 identity contract, active UUID person, matching context person, supported organization roles and invitation shape. Personal-only goes to `/account`; a membership or invitation goes to `/contexts` with personal still the default. Unavailable or suspended identity gets no local session and a distinct explanatory page. This affects first-party web routing, not a peer's own callback or app admission. |
| Multiple-person and multiple-entity UX | A first-party account can explicitly request fresh IAM authentication to sign in as another person; a completed sign-in clears the previous selected organization. The account overview shows the authenticated person's active organization roles and pending invitation count, with management/choice links. Changing an organization context still keeps the same human. It does not silently select an organization, infer resource permissions, or merge people. |
| Restricted state and roster consistency | First-party account pages distinguish a 403 suspended identity from a signed-out user. Protected API responses are `no-store`. Organization detail holds a shared row lock through the membership and roster read against API writers that lock the row for update. |

Local verification: web tests 12/12 passed (including personal, member, pending invite, suspended, mismatched, malformed and offline post-auth cases); reference consumer tests 12/12 passed (linking, local grant, selected membership and revocation); API tests 3 passed, 4 database-dependent tests skipped because `TEST_DATABASE_URL` was unavailable in the local environment. Web and API typecheck/build passed; the Next build enumerated the login/callback/account/contexts/organization routes. A CI PostgreSQL run and a real deployed, signed-in browser pass are still required. No staging deployment or live email/organization/peer acceptance is claimed by these local results.

**Completeness:** AccessLobby now distinguishes human identity from personal and provisional organization contexts and routes those humans accordingly in the first-party web flow. It does **not** yet distinguish, authenticate, authorize and route *every source-mentioned account class end-to-end*. Machine actors, verified controllers, households, hierarchy, app-entry grants, recovery/closure and real peer adoption have no approved complete policy/implementation. Source Pack Q-004 through Q-010, Q-019 through Q-021 and Q-026 are decision gates, not permission to invent one account model for all actors.
