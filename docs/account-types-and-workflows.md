# Account actors and workflows

This catalogue translates the supplied AccessLobby sources into product boundaries and an implementation sequence. It is not a claim that every historical proposal is an approved account type or a deployed capability. The direct August 7 owner prompt (`SRC-0026`, sections 5, 8–11 and 24), transfer-kit D-008/D-009, Source Pack decisions and conflicts, AccessLobby 1/2 excerpts, MVP history, and UI chat/images were checked. The UI images are illustrative designs; their verification scores, organizations, app permissions, devices and profile details are sample content, not live data. `PROJECT-CONSTITUTION.md` and the current integration contract govern the implementation.

## Identity, context, role and account are different things

| Concept | Canonical owner | Current treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Human/person | AccessLobby durable UUID; Keycloak authenticates the human | Implemented. Person is created on first valid issuer subject resolution. Email and OIDC `sub` are not the durable ID. |
| Personal participation | Same human | Implemented as the default, always available context. No separate personal user record. |
| Business/organization | AccessLobby shared entity and membership | Implemented in this change as an MVP-provisional flat organization. It is never a login credential or a substitute for a person. No legal verification is inferred from creating it. |
| Organization role | AccessLobby membership | MVP-provisional `owner`, `administrator`, `member` for membership administration only. A person can have different roles in different organizations. |
| Acting context | AccessLobby web account view | MVP-provisional sealed, one-hour selection tied to person ID, rechecked against active membership on every view. This is **not** an OIDC claim, peer authorization grant, or delegation token. |
| Application account/admission | Each peer owns its local account/resource roles; AccessLobby may eventually own broad app grants | Existing `/v1/me` resolution and explicit peer linking remain unchanged. Organization membership alone admits no user to a peer. |
| Entitlement/license/payment | Specialist billing/commerce system, with future projection | No entitlement inferred from organization or role. |

The first-party organization API requires a validated `accesslobby-api` token from `accesslobby-web`. Peer clients can continue to call `/v1/me`, but cannot call these management routes. The authenticated bearer subject, never a submitted actor ID, determines the person. Membership operations use database checks and audit events. A user cannot view another organization's roster, change a role outside their membership, or remove the last owner. An administrator can invite/remove members; only an owner can appoint or remove administrators and owners. These policy choices are **MVP-PROVISIONAL**, not a settled platform-wide role taxonomy.

## Human workflows available in this change

| Participant | Entry and continuing workflow | Exit and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Individual using AccessLobby personally | Register/sign in through the existing Keycloak flow; `/v1/me` resolves one stable person ID; `/contexts` begins at “For yourself”. Repeat sign-in preserves the ID. | Existing scoped sign-out. Suspension denies identity resolution. Full recovery, deletion and merge policy remain separate work. |
| Organization creator/owner | Sign in personally, create a named organization, receive its first active owner membership. Invite another active AccessLobby person by exact person ID. View roster, assign an existing member owner/admin/member, or revoke invitations. | Cannot leave, be removed, or demote themselves as the only owner. App entry is separate. No corporate ownership or legal controller verification is asserted. |
| Invited administrator | Existing person signs in; invitation targeted to their ID appears in their account for seven days; they accept or decline. Once active, they can invite or remove ordinary members. | Cannot grant administrator/owner or remove either. Owner can revoke an unaccepted invitation or remove/change the administrator. |
| Invited member | Same person-specific accept/decline flow, then can select the organization context and see its members. May belong to many organizations and switch without changing their human sign-in. | Can leave; owner/admin may remove them. A removed person loses that context at the next account view, and a new invitation is required to rejoin. |
| Existing person newly invited | Share their AccessLobby ID with an owner/admin through an independently trusted channel. No email-to-person guessing or automatic membership. | Invitations expire after seven days, can be revoked, and require an authenticated response from the intended person. |
| New person intended for an organization | Register and resolve their person ID first, then share it with the inviter; invitation/acceptance follows the existing-person flow. | Email-address invitations, pre-registration reservations and mail delivery require verified-contact binding and abuse controls that are not defined yet. |

The “current context” control in `/contexts` changes only the AccessLobby web view. It does not alter an OIDC token, authenticate an organization as a human, choose a peer account, or confer a right to perform a peer operation. A later peer context contract must verify the actor, active membership, app admission and peer-local permission at the point of use, with revocation and audit semantics.

## Other source-mentioned participants and their safe workflows

| Source-mentioned type | Identity and intended workflow | Current disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Worker, employee, contractor, vendor, supplier, seller, developer, manager, department head, finance/procurement/sales/warehouse/support/auditor | A human uses their own durable person ID. They can join one or more organizations through invitation. Their job title, departmental scope, seller/supplier profile, employment state and transaction permissions belong to the relevant organization or peer. | Person + organization membership works now. These are **not** ten extra global login types or universal roles. Peer-specific onboarding and offboarding remain with peers. See `SRC-0026` section 24 and AccessLobby-2 paragraphs 8667–8717. |
| Controller, delegated administrator, billing administrator, security administrator | Human with a potentially distinct verified authority relationship and narrower power. Establish authority, evidence, expiry, step-up and revocation before privileged changes; log original actor and effective authority. | Proposed concepts, not aliases for owner/admin. Do not grant these titles by self-attestation or by a generic membership. `SRC-0026` section 10; Q-004/Q-006. |
| Multiple businesses or external partner organization | One human may create/join several separately identified organizations. External organizations follow the same identity-before-organization principle, subject to future verification and ownership policy. | Flat memberships work now. Parent/child entities, legal claims, external tenant isolation, invite-gated app entry and billing remain open. |
| Household, family or informal group | A person participates personally and might represent an informal group with its own consent, membership and safeguarding rules. | Source mentions a context example, but no approved lifecycle/authority model. Do not disguise a household as a verified business or give a minor an organization-owner workflow. Q-004. |
| Guest or anonymous visitor | A peer may offer guest-first browsing or a local guest basket. If the person elects to register, resolve a new human identity and explicitly link peer state after proof of control. | Peer-local guest state only; no anonymous AccessLobby identity or automatic email merge. WordPress customer/staff/guest rules Q-017 remain open. |
| WordPress staff/admin, customer and guest | Prior owner direction requires staff/admin SSO, allows customer SSO to be optional during transition, and preserves guest checkout. Existing WordPress users link after proving both accounts; WordPress roles remain local. Administrative entry needs an explicit allowlist, MFA/step-up and audit. | This is a WordPress adapter policy, not a new AccessLobby role or automatic permission from an organization membership. The concrete connector is not implemented here; Source Pack Q-017 tracks its rollout details. |
| Peer-local customer, merchant, supplier or legacy account | Peer authenticates its existing account; user then separately signs in to AccessLobby; peer atomically links local ID to issuer/sub/person ID after both proofs, preserving peer history and local roles. | Existing reference-consumer demo and contract. Never infer a link from email or organization name. |
| Discovered external organization or facility | A peer records the discovered entity and its provenance without implying it has authenticated or claimed an AccessLobby organization. A future claim flow verifies authority and links records with audit/review. | No canonical AccessLobby login or auto claim. Source Pack Q-026. |
| Application/OIDC client | Registered by an operator with exact redirect/logout URIs, client ID, audience, PKCE/client authentication as applicable. A human may enter via that client and resolve their own person ID. | Existing consumer onboarding. Client ID is not a human, organization membership or service principal. |
| Service principal, workload and API integration | An organization/operator registers a separate non-human principal, assigns bounded application audience/scope and accountable owner, provisions rotating credentials, logs usage, suspends/revokes, and handles owner departure. | Design workflow only. No AccessLobby machine credential issuer, scope contract, approval or revocation policy exists. Existing infrastructure service accounts remain distinct. Q-007/Q-019. |
| Device | Enroll with proof of possession and accountable human/organization; bind hardware/session where applicable; rotate/revoke and preserve device audit. | Trusted login device and independent device identity are different models; neither is implemented as a global account. Q-007/Q-021. |
| AI agent/automation bot | Register a distinct non-human actor and its human/organization sponsor; bind explicit purpose, audience, expiry, scopes and approvals; log original human, agent and effective authority for every delegated action; stop on revocation. | No human impersonation token or unbounded inherited organization role. Delegation and service credential semantics await Q-006/Q-007. |

Switching **context** keeps the same authenticated human and selects a membership. Switching **accounts** to a different human requires a fresh authenticated principal and cannot be implemented by changing an organization cookie. The sources also envision departments, branches, teams and subsidiaries; the flat organization in this iteration intentionally does not invent their hierarchy or inherit rights between them.

## Lifecycle and next contracts

1. **Now:** the migration adds organizations, memberships, seven-day person-targeted invitations and auditable organization events. Creation, acceptance, role change, revocation, removal and exit are first-party operations. Deactivated/suspended persons cannot use the authenticated API. The context selection is local web state and is invalidated by membership checks or sign-out.
2. **Next human account contracts:** verified contact binding for invitation before registration, organizational legal/control verification, multi-owner recovery and transfer, suspended organization/person lifecycle, app admission and context-aware peer contract, notification delivery and rate limits. The current exact-ID invitation flow deliberately avoids claiming to solve these.
3. **Later actor contracts:** household safeguards, enterprise federation and organizational hierarchy; dedicated machine/device/service/agent principals with credential rotation, delegation lineage and revocation; explicit discovered-entity claim and peer guest migration. Each needs a separate owner decision and security model before becoming a login or live authority.
